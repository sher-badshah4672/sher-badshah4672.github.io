Welcome

I am a PhD candidate at Dalhousie University, working in the HyperMatrix Lab with Prof. Hassan Sajjad. My research asks when we can trust large language models as judges. Automatic evaluation now drives model development, yet LLM judges can be biased, inconsistent, and confidently wrong. I build methods that make their verdicts more reliable: aggregating multiple judges, grounding verdicts in retrieved evidence, and escalating hard cases only when needed.

A second line of my work gives these judges formal guarantees. Using uncertainty estimation and conformal risk control, a judge can abstain or retrieve more evidence when it is unsure, while keeping the error rate on the verdicts it does return below a level the user chooses. My thesis, Improving the Reliability of Large Language Models as Judges, brings these pieces together. Before NLP, I worked on empirical software engineering, with papers on DevOps, software processes, and AI ethics.
