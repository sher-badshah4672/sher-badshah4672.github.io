Welcome

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at Dalhousie University, where I work in the HyperMatrix Lab with Prof. Hassan Sajjad. My research is about making large language models reliable. A model that is right most of the time is still hard to deploy if we cannot tell when it is wrong. I study how to measure what a model does not know and how to act on that signal.

Much of my work is in uncertainty quantification. I develop uncertainty estimates for LLM outputs and combine them with conformal prediction, so a system can answer, seek more evidence, or abstain while keeping its error rate below a level the user sets. I have applied these ideas to LLM evaluation, where they come with formal guarantees on the decisions a model accepts.

I am also interested in weight-space programming, a new area that reads, edits, and transfers what a network has learned by working on its weights directly. My current project explores whether a skill learned by one model can be found in its weights and moved into another. Another interest is reinforcement learning and post-training of language models. Before NLP, I worked on empirical software engineering, with papers on DevOps, software processes, and AI ethics.
